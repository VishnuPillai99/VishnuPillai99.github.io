TrackEats — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2 October 2026 · Applies to TrackEats for Android (package io.github.vishnupillai99.trackeats)

TrackEats estimates the calories and macronutrients in a meal. This policy describes what happens to your information. It is written to be accurate rather than reassuring: where something leaves your phone, it says so plainly.

The short version

TrackEats has no account and no TrackEats server. Your food log, photos, weight, water, goals and profile are stored only on your phone.

Meal photos, food searches and any tips or diet plans you ask for are sent to Google's Gemini API using an API key you create yourself. They do not pass through any computer belonging to the developer of TrackEats.

Uninstalling the app deletes everything, permanently. There is no backup and nobody — including the developer — can recover it.

Who is responsible

TrackEats is published by an independent developer. Questions about this policy, or about your data, go to vipillainewai99@gmail.com. This is the only contact route, and it is checked.

What leaves your phone

1. Meal photographs, sent to Google

When you photograph a meal — or pick an existing photo — the image is sent to Google's Gemini API to be identified. The request contains the image and a short text instruction, and nothing else: no name, no weight, no goals, no history, and no identifier for you or your phone.

2. Food searches, sent to Google

Searching for a food by name sends the words you typed to the same Google API, so it can return nutrition figures for that food.

3. Health tips, diet plans and exercise suggestions, sent to Google

These three optional features are generated by the same Google API, and to produce anything useful they send the figures they are based on: your sex, age, height, weight, activity level, goal, calorie target, your recent average calorie intake, and any dietary preferences you entered. Your name, your photos and your individual meals are not sent. If you would rather these figures never left the phone, do not use these three features — everything else in the app works without them.

Your own key, and what Google may do with what it receives

Every request above is authenticated with an API key that you created in Google AI Studio and pasted into the app. Your use of the Gemini API is a matter between you and Google, under Google's own terms, not ours:

Gemini API Additional Terms of Service · Google Privacy Policy

Note in particular that on Google's free (unpaid) tier, Google states that it uses content submitted through the API to improve and develop Google's products and machine-learning technologies, and that human reviewers may read, annotate and process that content. Google's terms also advise not submitting sensitive, confidential or personal information to the unpaid tier. TrackEats cannot change this, cannot switch it off, and does not control it. If that is not acceptable to you, do not use the photo, search, tips, plan or exercise features — barcode scanning and manual entry never contact Google at all.

4. Barcodes, sent to Open Food Facts

Scanning a barcode sends the barcode number — and nothing else — to Open Food Facts, a free public food database, to look up the product. No key, no account, nothing about you. Open Food Facts privacy policy.

That is the complete list

Those four are every case in which TrackEats sends anything anywhere. There is no other network traffic and no TrackEats server to send it to.

Health Connect and your step count

If you allow it, TrackEats reads one thing from Android's Health Connect: your total step count for today. It is shown on the Home screen and stored on the phone with the rest of your log.

  • TrackEats never writes anything to Health Connect and reads no other data type.
  • Your step count is never transmitted — not to Google's Gemini API, not to anyone. It is not included in tips, diet plans or exercise suggestions.
  • Step data is never used for advertising, never sold or transferred to anyone, and never used to assess your employment, insurance or creditworthiness.
  • The permission is optional. The app works fully without it, and you can withdraw it at any time in the Health Connect app or in Android Settings → Health Connect → App permissions → TrackEats. Withdrawing it stops the card updating; step figures already stored on the phone are removed when you clear the app's data or uninstall it.

Children and age

TrackEats is not directed at children and is intended for adults. Calorie and weight guidance is not appropriate for children. In addition, Google's Gemini API terms — which you accept when you create the API key the app needs — require you to be 18 or older.

Your data: exporting and deleting it

  • Export — Menu → Your data → Export my data writes everything out as a single JSON file you can save or send yourself. Your API key is not included in the export.
  • Delete everything — Android Settings → Apps → TrackEats → Storage → Clear storage, or simply uninstall the app. Either removes your entire log, your photos, your profile and your API key from the phone, irreversibly.

There is no account to close and no server-side copy to request the deletion of, because neither exists. If you want your key to stop working for Google as well, delete it in Google AI Studio, which is outside TrackEats' control.

Security

Every request the app makes is over HTTPS. Your API key is stored with Android's keystore-backed secure storage rather than in ordinary app storage. Your log itself is held in the app's private storage, which other apps on a non-rooted device cannot read. No copy of it exists anywhere else.

Changes to this policy

If the policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. A sign-in option for backing up and syncing your data between devices is being considered but is not part of the app today; if it is ever added, this policy will be rewritten to describe it, and what is stored on a server, before that version ships.

Not medical advice

TrackEats is not a medical device. It does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any medical condition. Calorie estimates from a photograph are approximations, and BMI, BMR, TDEE, body-fat and ideal-weight figures are formulas applied to numbers you typed in, not measurements. Tips, diet plans and exercise suggestions are generated by a language model and may be wrong. Do not use any of it to make a medical decision; speak to a qualified professional.
